Research

Research Interests

My research broadly focuses on contaminant fate and transport, reactive transport modeling, and remediation of natural and engineered environmental systems. I am interested in combining physical modeling, numerical simulation, laboratory experiments, and data-driven approaches to better understand contaminant behavior and improve remediation design.

  • PFAS fate and transport in groundwater
  • Reactive transport modeling in porous media
  • Competitive adsorption and contaminant–media interactions
  • Groundwater remediation and adsorptive barriers
  • Multidimensional environmental simulation and remediation optimization

PFAS Transport and Competitive Adsorption

This work investigates how PFAS mixtures move through porous media and interact with adsorptive materials. I use batch adsorption experiments, column transport studies, and reactive transport models to evaluate how competitive adsorption affects contaminant breakthrough, retention, and displacement. This research helps clarify when single-solute adsorption data are sufficient and when mixture effects become important for predicting PFAS transport.

Reactive Transport Modeling in Porous Media

I develop and apply numerical models to simulate contaminant transport, adsorption, mass transfer, and breakthrough behavior in porous media. My current work combines laboratory observations with one-dimensional transport models, with broader interest in extending these approaches to two- and three-dimensional environmental systems.

Groundwater Remediation and Barrier Design

This research focuses on using reactive transport modeling to evaluate groundwater remediation strategies, including adsorptive barriers for PFAS-impacted plumes. I am interested in connecting laboratory-scale measurements with field-scale simulations to support practical remediation design, performance evaluation, and optimization.
