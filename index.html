Research Highlights

Mass transfer limitations control PFAS breakthrough

Colloidal activated carbon (CAC) is injected into an aquifer to form a porous barrier that slows PFAS migration. Barriers are commonly sized from equilibrium sorption capacity, which assumes PFAS reaches the carbon surface as fast as groundwater carries it through. At the grain scale, PFAS must first transfer from the flow paths onto CAC surfaces, and this transfer takes time. When it is slow relative to the groundwater residence time in the barrier, PFAS breaks through while much of the sorption capacity remains unused.

The Damköhler number provides a framework for comparing these two time scales. It is defined as the ratio of the advective residence time tA to the grain-scale mass transfer time tR, and the dashed Da = 1 line separates kinetic control (upper left) from local equilibrium (lower right). Column conditions fall on the kinetic side (a), so an equilibrium model does not reproduce the measured profiles. At the field scale (b), natural head gradients fall near equilibrium, and these mass transfer limitations are not important. They become important only under active pumping, when groundwater residence times in the barrier are short.

Where the carbon sits matters more than how much

Under natural head gradients, grain-scale mass transfer is not the limiting process at the field scale. Spatial structure is. In a heterogeneous aquifer, the plume does not reach the barrier as a uniform front. Hydraulic conductivity organizes the flow into fast channels (top), and PFOA follows them (bottom), arriving at the barrier, marked by the dashed lines, concentrated in a few narrow paths.

Placement therefore matters. The two barriers above hold the same total mass of CAC. In the left panel the carbon lies within the fast-flow paths marked by the cyan contour. In the right panel it lies away from them and leaves a high-flux window uncovered.

These windows control performance. The same total carbon mass spans roughly a threefold range in breakthrough time, with the misaligned case (green) breaking through first and the uniform case (blue) last. Adding carbon mass delays breakthrough but does not remove the window. Only redistributing capacity into the window does.
